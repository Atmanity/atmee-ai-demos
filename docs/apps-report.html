Mini-apps on the Avatar API

The fourteen in-call mini-apps from Atmanity/apps, rebuilt on the public Avatar API alone, and each run live on staging. This report says what each app is for, how it is built, and what the API could not do for it. The five earlier sample apps are at the end.

  • 14live runs on staging
  • 3,389tests, all passing
  • 9API features still missing
  • 5classic apps kept

Summary

What the API is missing

Every app follows one rule: the app decides, the avatar performs. The app owns the rules, the facts and the screen, and tells the avatar what is true (state), what happened (event) and how to behave (note). That works well for anything the app can tell the avatar before the user speaks. Answers staged in state shaped the avatar's immediate reply in every app: a star's rising time, the next line of working, the pot, a refusal, an emergency sentence.

It breaks down for anything the app learns after the user speaks. The avatar has already started its reply about a second before the app hears the transcript, so it fills the gap. It says "let's see" and waits for an outcome that may never come, or it invents one: a wrong verdict, a made-up sight, a made-up room size.

Update, 23 October: function calling shipped on staging the morning after these runs. POST /v1/session now takes appTools; the avatar calls the page over a LiveKit RPC and, for an await tool, holds its reply for the answer. A probe proved it: asked for a secret number only the page knew, the avatar called the tool and said the number in its first reply. The kit supports it; the fourteen apps still use the workaround and are the next thing to convert.

How every app is built

One server, one room kit, one shape per app

Every app is a page and a few server routes in one repository. Nothing uses Atmee internals: a server holding the API key, a page in the LiveKit room the API returns, and the room's text streams.

The app decides

Rules, scores, facts and plans live in the app's engine, ported from the Python originals with their tests. Secrets (the city, hidden cards, the next line of working) stay on the server; the page sees a masked view.

The avatar performs

It is told what is on screen (state, up to 3,858 characters), its standing rules (notes, 2,000 characters each) and what just happened (events, spoken when it has the floor).

Three ways in

Buttons act at once. Typed lines are judged before the avatar hears them. Speech reaches the app about a second after the avatar started replying, so the answer is staged in advance where it can be.

Proven live

A test harness feeds the room a synthetic microphone from spoken WAV files. Three shared staging avatars let three apps run at once; each README records its run.

Guess the City

A photo of a mystery city, three clues, and a host who never knows the answer.

apps/guess-the-city/14 tests

What it is for

A geography quiz. Three cities a game; a city guessed before any clue is worth 4 points, and each clue costs one. Say or type a guess, ask for a clue, give up or move on. This was the reference port the other thirteen followed.

How it is built

  • The game runs on the server, because the city is the secret: the page only ever sees a masked view (no name, country or fun fact before the reveal).
  • Gemini writes a fresh three-city deck per game; Wikipedia's lead image is served from a random token so the file name cannot give the city away.
  • Guesses are judged by the app's own model call (misheard names count); command words (clue, give up, next) by rules.
  • The next clue is staged in state, so a spoken "can I have a clue?" gets the real one at once. Verdicts and reveals go out as events.

What the API missed

  • SeenReplies before the verdict

    "Is it Paris?" got "That's not quite right…" in every run, before the app had judged it, despite a note forbidding it.

  • SeenInvents clues unless they are staged

    Before staging, "can I have a clue?" got a made-up clue ("home to the Burj Khalifa" for New York) in 2 of 3 runs. Staging fixed it.

  • SeenGreets as the test avatar

    "I'm Emily, a livestream host…" before the app's welcome.

  • SeenCheck-in while the player studies the photo

    "Take your time, or just let me know if you want another clue!"

  • ExpectedNo speech hints for city names

    Rare names rely on the recogniser; the model judge absorbs some mishearings.

Live run

Staging, avatar 2cd3ec08: real clue on request, "I give up" revealed Bangkok, typed "What is my score?" answered from the board. A second run proved the mid-session mic switch (65 s billed).

Chess Opponent

A real game of chess against an opponent who moves on its own, says why, and needles you a little.

apps/chess-opponent/431 tests

What it is for

Play a full game of chess against the avatar. Drag a piece or say or type a move ("e4", "knight to f3", "castle"); pick a side and a difficulty, resign or start over. The opponent explains its moves and names openings.

How it is built

  • The board runs in the page with chess.js: chess has no secrets, and drags must be instant.
  • The opponent is a one-move-ahead heuristic with three strengths and a seeded tie-break, ported from the Python original (with its "1.e4 h5" bug fixed).
  • Rules read spoken moves conservatively; doubtful lines go to the server's model judge, whose move must land on a square the player actually said.
  • state carries the player's legal moves and staged answers to "can I play black?" and "go easier", so the avatar's first reply is true.

What the API missed

  • HeldReplies before the board acts

    "Rook to a five" was refused twice: first from the staged legal moves, then by the app's event. Nothing illegal was ever agreed to.

  • SeenCommentary dropped

    After the switch to Black, the opponent's first move (1.Nf3) was never named; the avatar only said "It's your turn".

  • SeenCheck-in hurries a thinking player

    "Take your time…", "No rush at all…". The original waited 45 s.

  • SeenGreets as the test avatar

    The board labels it "Opponent" because the avatar keeps its own name.

  • ExpectedNo speech hints for squares

    "b4" can come back as "before"; every square was heard right in this run.

Live run

Staging, avatar baf61f2c, 233 s: e4, Nf3, an illegal move refused, a switch to Black, beginner mode, undo refused, typed d5, resignation. A first attempt crashed from a dev-server bundling bug, since fixed.

Poker Table

Heads-up no-limit hold'em against the avatar: say your moves, it plays its own.

apps/poker-table/158 tests

What it is for

Heads-up Texas Hold'em: 1,000 chips each, blinds 10/20, played until one stack is empty. Play with buttons or say "call", "raise to 60", "I fold", "all in". The avatar plays its own hand and needles you about yours.

How it is built

  • The whole table runs on the server: hold'em rules, a hand evaluator checked against seven golden hands, and the match. The avatar's hidden cards never reach the page or the avatar.
  • The opponent decides on the server with a model call and a 4.5 s rule-based fallback.
  • A strict parser reads spoken moves (number words included) and asks rather than guesses; every amount is checked by the table.
  • The table's rules travel as four notes (about 4,250 characters); rulings and refusals go out as events; the pot and the player's cards sit in state.

What the API missed

  • SeenA holding line nobody kept

    "Raise to five" arrived as "Raised to five.", which the parser missed; the avatar had said "let's see" and nothing followed. Parser fixed.

  • SeenAn event and a question collide

    A typed "what beats what?" met the opponent's move event; only the event was answered.

  • SeenCheck-in while sizing a bet

    "Still thinking over that forty to call, or are…"

  • SeenGreets as the test avatar

    It described its own test setup ("a fixed four hundred millisecond prime lead").

  • ExpectedRulings paraphrased

    Live rulings kept their numbers; the table's message line shows the exact range.

Live run

Staging, avatar 2cd3ec08, 198 s: rules from state, a call answered by the opponent's raise, pot and cards read right, a fold with the uncalled bet returned, hand two dealt.

Stroke Tutor

A calligraphy teacher who grades your stroke order and direction, never your handwriting.

apps/stroke-tutor/181 tests

What it is for

Write a character one stroke at a time and get graded on stroke order and direction. Seven characters from 一 to 永, plus the capitals A and E for learners with no Japanese. Two misses and the teacher shows the stroke.

How it is built

  • Lessons and reference strokes stay on the server: an unrevealed stroke is the secret, and the page sees a masked snapshot.
  • The grader is pure geometry, ported from the Python original; 12,000 random drawings got identical verdicts from both.
  • The canvas turns ink into grid points (1,000 units across); each gesture is one stroke sent to the lesson.
  • A coach queue holds cues while the avatar speaks and keeps only the newest, so coaching does not pile up behind a sentence.

What the API missed

  • HeldAnswers "which way does it go?" before the reveal

    It said only "let me show you", as the notes ask; describing the hidden stroke from memory would be the leak this app prevents.

  • SeenCheck-in interrupts drawing

    Twice in one run: "Whenever you're ready, try stroke two!"

  • SeenMisreads a correct state

    After stroke 2 was missed twice and shown, it said "We've got the first two down".

  • SeenSpoken commands wait for the transcript

    "Next" and "start over" act a second late; the buttons always work.

  • ExpectedStale coaching

    An event cannot be withdrawn once sent, so a cue can arrive after the learner has drawn again.

Live run

Staging, avatar baf61f2c, 151 s: holding line then lesson, a wrong-direction stroke caught twice and shown, "start over" returned to stroke 1.

BLS Tutor

A calm paramedic walks you through CPR and first-aid scenarios, step by step.

apps/bls-tutor/245 tests

What it is for

Basic Life Support practice: an adult collapse, a child at a pool, choking at dinner, the recovery position. Say what you would do; the drill grades it against the AHA/ERC sequence with the exact numbers. If it sounds like a real emergency, a red Call 911 / 112 card covers the screen.

How it is built

  • The drill runs on the server and a rules-only judge grades answers: no model decides whether CPR was right.
  • Only the live step's answer, teaching line and hint are staged in state, fenced by "only if they ask"; steps ahead never are.
  • Emergency phrases are caught in the page itself, so the red card goes up even with the server down.
  • A standing note carries the exact emergency sentence, so the avatar's own first reply already has it.

What the API missed

  • SeenThe emergency line cannot be forced

    "My dad just collapsed" got the line from the note, behind the platform's filler ("Man, that's rough, Stop — …call 911"), then the event said it a second time.

  • SeenTranscripts lose words

    "This is too hard, can I pick a different scenario?" arrived as "I pick a different scenario?" (it still worked).

  • HeldNo verdict before the reply

    Both answers got "let's see what the drill says", then the right verdict.

  • SeenCheck-in prods a learner

    Three times in one run.

  • KnownNo push-to-answer

    Muting the avatar would also stop the transcript the drill needs.

Live run

Staging, avatar baf61f2c, 300 s: choking scenario graded step by step, "show me" answered from the staged answer, a scenario switch, the emergency card up the moment the transcript arrived, then cleared on "false alarm".

Quadratic Whiteboard

Name a quadratic, pick a method, and reveal the working one line at a time beside its graph.

apps/quadratic-whiteboard/304 tests

What it is for

A whiteboard a teacher drives by voice in front of a class. Put up a quadratic, choose factoring, completing the square or the formula, and say "next" for each line of working beside the parabola. Every number is exact: surds stay surds.

How it is built

  • The engine runs on the server in exact fractions and matches the Python original line for line on 1,210 equations; unrevealed working never leaves it.
  • Only the next line's sentence is staged in state, so a spoken "next" is read correctly at once; spoken requests get no event, buttons and typed lines do.
  • Spoken maths ("x squared minus four x plus three") is read by rules; anything else goes to a model call.
  • The avatar is "the chalk hand": it reads lines and answers questions about the board, and never teaches or reads ahead.

What the API missed

  • HeldReplies before the board moves

    Every "next" and "go on" was answered with the right staged line while the board moved to match.

  • SeenSpeaks into the class's pauses

    "Whenever you are ready for the next line…", three times; the original never spoke unprompted.

  • SeenGreets as the test avatar

    A lighthouse keeper on the Taitung coast, then the board's opening.

  • ExpectedOne unrevealed line is within reach

    Staging puts one line in the avatar's state; no early read was seen.

  • ExpectedDecimals instead of surds

    Asked for a decimal, it kept "two plus root three".

Live run

Staging, avatar 26cbe079, 267 s: factoring revealed by voice, button and typing; discriminant and roots answered; factoring refused for x² − 4x + 1; the formula started.

Trajectory Coach

Shoot at things on a range drawn to scale; a coach explains, in real numbers, why it fell short.

apps/trajectory-coach/228 tests

What it is for

Learn projectile motion by shooting: aim by dragging an arrow or saying "forty-five at twenty-four", fire, and the coach explains the miss in real numbers. Six challenges, each built to disprove a piece of folklore about 45°.

How it is built

  • The flight engine runs in the page, ported line for line; a golden test compares 273 graded shots byte for byte with the Python output.
  • The range itself lands each shot on a timer, and the verdict stays out of state while the ball flies.
  • Rules read numbers, "X at Y" and relative steps; shots described by their shape ("like a mortar") go to a model call.
  • The launcher's limits and the challenges are staged; the landing line is the main event.

What the API missed

  • SeenActs out what the app did not do

    "Lob it like a mortar", heard as "Lobbied like a mortar.", got "let's send it!" while the range did nothing. The model prompt was fixed.

  • SeenPredicts the result before landing

    "let's watch this one fly right over!" before the shot came down (right by luck).

  • SeenRefusals said twice

    "maxes out at eighty-five degrees" from the staged limits, then the event.

  • SeenCheck-in before the opening

    "Just checking in—did you want to keep chatting…" before the app's welcome was said.

  • ExpectedNumbers paraphrased

    Both landing lines kept every figure ("0.76 m going past 40 m, 2.24 m below the top").

Live run

Staging, avatar 2cd3ec08, 224 s: a miss explained from its numbers, a steeper aim, 90° refused, a clear at 45° and 24 m/s, the Moon challenge.

    Live facts were the catalogue's; nothing gates the avatar's memory.

    None in a run with steady questions; it cannot be turned off.

Home Planner

Plan a whole home by talking: rooms, walls, doors and furniture at real sizes.

apps/room-planner/238 tests

What it is for

Plan a home of up to two floors with an architect: give the size and bedrooms, then trade area between rooms, move walls, add doors and windows, furnish at real sizes, save and compare schemes, undo, and download the drawing sheet.

How it is built

  • The full Python engine is ported (grid, rooms, placement, generator, checks, schemes, SVG sheet) and runs in the page, so drags are instant and undo is local.
  • Rules handle trades and common commands at once; other lines go to a model call that returns operations by room and piece id, never coordinates.
  • A critic folds the plan's new problems into the next line (a windowless study, a bed reachable from one side).
  • Photos are read by the app's own model into catalogue pieces.

What the API missed

  • SeenA made-up size before the real one

    "Give the study two square metres off the kitchen" got "eight point three square metres", then the app's 8.4.

  • SeenMixes units

    After the board switched to ft², "the stairs… at thirty-nine square feet, leaving fifty-nine square metres".

  • SeenAsks what the app already did

    "Add an upstairs" got "Are you thinking of a whole second floor?" while the floor was already added.

  • KnownCannot see the photo

    Only what the app's reader extracted.

  • SeenCheck-in while studying the plan

    The original waited 40 s and nudged at most twice.

Staging, avatar 26cbe079, 285 s: a 63 m² seven-room plan, two trades, a queen bed placed and undone, a scheme saved with open plan, square feet, an upper floor.

What they found missing

  • No audience in the room: one user token per session, no viewer tokens or egress, so a stream must re-broadcast the avatar.
  • Sessions are capped by plan (900 s on Free, up to 4 h), and an avatar runs one session at a time.
  • A typed turn sent while the avatar is speaking was dropped, not queued (the kit now holds typed turns until the avatar finishes).
  • No transcript or reply feed outside the room; the app must read the room's transcription stream.
  • The check-in and the persona are the avatar's, as in the mini-apps.
